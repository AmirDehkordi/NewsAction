Can AI models that read financial news anticipate how stock prices will react, or tell when the market has over- or under-reacted? We test this with AI decision models, non-generative models that return probabilities for typed questions, in a pre-registered, look-ahead-free design on S&P 500 stocks. We fine-tune an open-weights decision model on 2024-2025 news and returns, compare it with commercial decision models and standard baselines, mask company identities, freeze the analysis plan with a cryptographic hash, and unblind a January-July 2026 test period once. The fine-tuned model's signal replicated on two validation periods (daily information coefficient 0.065, t = 3.6) but fell to 0.020 (t = 1.7) on the blinded test; none of the five primary hypotheses is supported. Where the models predict anything, it is the overnight gap: commercial models forecast the move to the open (information coefficient 0.074 to 0.077) but not the move after it. The models cannot tell over- from under-reaction, and no pre-registered trading strategy survives transaction costs and a deflated Sharpe ratio. Commercial models identify masked companies in 62% to 74% of cases against a 28% baseline, a warning for backtests of language models on historical news. In an exploratory analysis, conditions selected on two validation periods, with in-sample Sharpe ratios up to 6.8, failed on the test. The market reads the news first.
