# NA-04 decisions — states: context, codebook, masking, question battery v1

Date: 2026-10-25. Branch: `feat/NA-04-states`. Self-audit: `artifacts/NA-04/REPORT.md`.
PREREG deviations: §15, ten NA-04 rows (appended in the working tree; operator to confirm).

## Pipeline and memory

- **One stage per process.** `python -m newsaction.states.build --stage context | mask | states | report`.
  Each stage reads the registered files of the earlier ones.
  - `context` (≈ 35 s, 0.7 GB): company context for every D0/D2 event key, market context per session,
    D2 reaction inputs for train / val-A / val-B.
  - `mask` (≈ 8 min, 2.1 GB; spaCy with 4 worker processes, so it must run as a module): masked title and
    snippet of the 296,082 event articles and the 3,062 A3.5 market articles, and the corpus surname list.
  - `states`: the JSON states and their token counts.
  - `report`: artifacts and the frozen hashes.
- **Context depends only on each key's own history.** `build.compute_context(keys)` gives the same rows for
  a subset of keys as for all of them (fixed session grid from `configs/splits.yaml`, per-symbol rolling
  windows). The determinism test (AC1) uses this to recompute context for a 2,000-row sample.
- **Inputs are registered files only** (CLAUDE.md F21): events, panel_public / panel_test_inputs (t−1
  columns), market_prices, fred_daily, cpi_vintages, earnings_dates, estimates_fy1, companies,
  symbol_tickers, articles_clean (`id`, `third_party_ok` only), market_articles. No sealed file, no lock call.

## Dependencies

- **spaCy English pipeline.** `en_core_web_sm` 3.8.0 is pinned in `pyproject.toml` (`embed` extra) by its
  release-wheel URL, with the hash in `uv.lock` (`uv sync --extra embed`). Only the `tok2vec` and `ner` pipes
  are loaded. This is one download from GitHub (explosion/spacy-models); no article text left the machine.
- **Tokenizer.** The NA-03 ModernBERT-large tokenizer (rev `45bb4654`), from the local cache.

## Codebook (S1; `configs/codebook_v1.yaml`, `states/codebook.py`)

- **Only moves change with the encoding.** E0–E4 apply to every move value (the stock's past-week /
  month / three-month abnormal performance, the six market series, the Call B reaction). Every other scale is
  words in all encodings. The NA-07 study therefore varies exactly one thing.
- **E3 multiples are truncated, not rounded.** All move edges lie on the 0.1 grid, so the shown multiple
  never contradicts its word (|z| = 1.97 is "moderate rise (≈1.9× …)"). E1 rounds ("+2.3σ"; "0.0σ" for
  |z| < 0.05).
- **Multi-day horizons.** Words use z_m{k} = ΣAR / (σ_cc·√k) (NA-01 momentum). The E3 noun is "a typical
  week's / month's / three-month move", which is what σ_cc·√k is, so the word and the multiple agree. Keys
  carry no digits (`past_week`, `past_month`, `past_three_months` for 5 / 20 / 60 sessions).
- **E0** is the raw change: the stock's raw return with the S&P 500 (SPY) return in parentheses, as in the
  PREREG example; percent for prices, basis points for rates, points for the VIX. Raw stock returns over
  5/20/60 sessions exist for public sessions only; E0 states are built only for the val samples.
- **E4** compares |value| with the same series' 252 previous values of the same horizon (≥ 126):
  "a rise larger than 97% of its daily moves in the past year".
- **Additions where §6.10 names a field but §6.11 has no scale** (PREREG §15 row 2): FY1 revision words
  (±1% / ±5% of the earlier consensus, floor $0.10), count words ("none" … "ten", "more than ten"), source
  type words from the domain class, "during today's trading session" (W2 news), the D1 time label "half an
  hour after the open" (no digits), volume vs normal on the attention-multiple scale, "not available".
- **Tier words contain digits** ("one of the 100 largest US companies", "size rank 101–250"). They are the
  exact PREREG §6.11 phrases and identical for every company of a tier, so AC4's digit checks remove these
  three fixed phrases first (REPORT §2, AC4).

## Context (S2; `states/context.py`, `states/macro.py`)

- **Company block.** Sector (GICS level 1); tier words; β_m words; volatility regime (σ_cc vs its previous
  252 values); performance vs the market and its sector over 5 / 20 / 60 sessions; FY1 EPS revision;
  earnings timing.
  - **No industry** (PREREG §15 row 1): ATOM has GICS level 1 only (NA-01 row). An industry level would also
    identify many companies.
  - **FY1** is the first annual period ending after the session. The consensus "now" is the last snapshot
    dated before the session; "before" is the last snapshot dated 31 days before the session or earlier.
  - **Earnings timing.** `earnings_ts` is naive; it is read as America/New_York wall time. A timestamp at or
    after a close belongs to the next session (16:00 means "after the close"), so the windows are
    [close(prev s), 09:15 ET] and [close(prev s), close(s)). The calendar was collected retrospectively
    (NA-00 contract); a report's date is treated as announced in advance.
  - Tickers map to companies through ATOM tickers, price symbols, ATOM `symbol_tickers`, the NA-02 vendor
    aliases and the curated tickers; ambiguous tickers are dropped.
- **Market block** (all through t−1). SPY previous-session move; VIX level percentile and change; 10-year
  yield change; high-yield OAS change; dollar and oil moves; scheduled releases today.
  - Each series is scored against its own 252 previous daily changes: z = change / (1.4826·MAD), with ≥ 126
    observations. In early 2024 the OAS history is too short (FRED's file starts 2023-08), so 18.5% of
    robustness-split sessions show "not available" for it.
  - **Publication lags.** market_prices closes and DGS10 are used as of the previous session (the Treasury
    publishes constant-maturity yields the same evening). The ICE BofA OAS is computed after the US close
    and reaches FRED later, so the state uses the change dated t−2 (`extra_lag: 1`).
  - **CPI release dates** are ALFRED vintage dates that add a new reference month; that leaves out the
    February seasonal re-vintages (2024-02-09). The October 2025 CPI was never published, and the
    data show it (2025-10-24 and 2025-12-18 releases).
  - **FOMC statement dates** come from the Fed calendar (URL in `configs/macro_calendar.yaml`, read
    2026-10-25). The 2025-08-22 notation vote is not a policy statement.
- **Attention block.** Articles vs the 60-session member mean (NA-03 `attention_ratio_*`) and distinct
  stories, of the state's own window (W0 for D0, W2 for D2).
- **Availability.** 45 new columns are registered in `configs/availability.yaml`: context `t_minus_1`
  (earnings flags `w0` / `w2`), macro flags `static`, reaction `d2_reaction`, masked text `w0`.

- **Aliases.** ATOM names (all share classes) with suffixes stripped (incl. "& Co.", "& Company", "Hldgs"),
  "(The)" / "(Eli)" moved to the front, leading prefixes of two or more words, and the first word alone when it is
  distinctive; plus curated names and tickers for the companies ranked in the top 150 in any month since 2024-01,
  renamed companies, and about 60 companies whose names were found right before their own "(TICKER)" in the event
  texts but were missing (Super Micro Computer, Wabtec, DuPont de Nemours, PSEG, Old Dominion Freight Line,
  L3Harris, Delta, Booking, Charles River, Southwest, Sands, …).
- **Matching.** Phrases and their distinctive words (4+ letters, not one of the common words or a legal
  suffix) match in any case, with spaces, hyphens and en dashes (or nothing: "MolsonCoors") and both apostrophes
  interchangeable; a capitalized name of 5+ letters also matches, as written, right after a lower-case letter or
  a digit ("Key TakeawaysHumana", "2025Principal"); a name's first word before a snippet's closing "..." counts
  ("estimates for Warner..."); a one-word
  name that is a common word ("Target", "Visa", "Delta") or shorter than 4 letters only as written, capitalized or
  in capitals. Products and subsidiaries that are not the company's name are not masked (PRD risks).
- **Tickers.** $T, any upper-case exchange code before a colon (NYSE:, BOATS:, XETRA:), full-width CJK forms
  ("（紐交所：DE）", "（HOOD US）"), class / venue suffixes (T.O, MS.N, COIN.US, T/PR), possessives (T's), option
  codes (ADI250703C305000, removed with the expiry and strike) and bare tickers; the bare-ticker stoplist holds only
  real English words and common upper-case abbreviations (61; ODFL, PAYC, IQV, NDAQ, … were wrongly listed
  before). A stoplisted ticker is masked with a prefix, in parentheses, as a possessive or before "stock" /
  "shares", and also bare in capitals unless the text is mostly in capitals (over 60% capital letters): in the
  companies' own articles the capitalized form is almost always the ticker (a sample of 40 was 40 ticker uses). Two
  exceptions keep the word: a clock time ("4:30 PM", Philip Morris) and IT before an IT noun ("IT spending", "IT
  services", Gartner). In a text mostly in capitals it is masked when it is not an ordinary lower-case word of the
  corpus ("IR"), stands in a list of tickers ("ALC, KEYS, TOL"), precedes a period label or an earnings word ("MAR
  Q2") or follows the company's name ("… INC /MD/ MAR:"). Bare tickers may carry digits ("ABBV19", "GE9X") and stand
  in pairs and lists ("Omnicom-IPG", "WMT/TGT/TJX") or right after a masked name ("Inc.ANET"); Reuters exchange
  letters (AAPL-Q, NKE-N) and drug codes (ABBV-400) count.
  Latin letters with accents count as letters, so "PMČR" (a Czech company) is not PM. Parenthesized tickers and
  quotes with a percent ("(COST 0.53%)", "MPC-1.32%", "(AAPL +0.87%)", "· IP. -0.58%", "FSLR0.00%",
  "|Price:49.32|Chg%:-0.60") are removed.
- **CJK company names.** About 1% of event articles contain Chinese, Japanese or Korean text. The CJK words right
  before a company's own ticker in parentheses ("迪爾股份（紐交所：DE）") are harvested from the event texts:
  Korean, the last word (seen twice); Japanese, the final katakana run; Chinese, the text after the last verb or
  function word ("美銀證券維持施貴寶" → 施貴寶), each also without corporate suffixes (股份, 公司, 集團, 科技,
  ・インク, …) and generic words. 541 names of 248 companies (`work/states/company_cjk_names.parquet`); they are
  masked in the company's own texts. A CJK name never written before the ticker is not known (NA-06 measures
  identifiability).
- **People** (PREREG §6.3 "person names", including surnames used alone). spaCy PERSON entities that are not a
  company alias, plus every capitalized occurrence of a corpus surname (the last word of a multi-word name seen ≥ 3
  times, not an ordinary word or a company-alias word; `work/states/person_surnames.parquet`) or of a listed public
  figure (`person_names`: heads of state, central bankers, officials, executives named by surname). A one-word NER
  entity counts only as such a surname, after a role noun or before a speech verb; an entity made only of ordinary
  lower-case words ("Balance Sheet", "Buy") is not a name; in a text that looks non-English only names of two or
  more capitalized words count. A name right after a role noun is dropped ("CEO Tim Cook said" → "CEO said");
  otherwise "an executive" with an executive title within six words, else "a person". Chinese transliterations
  of Western names (up to two characters, "·", up to four; "薩姆·阿爾特曼") become "a person".
- **Dates** → "recently", in English and in French, German, Italian, Spanish, Portuguese, Dutch and Polish (day +
  month, day ranges "July 17-18", month + year, weekdays, "am 21."), Chinese, Japanese and Korean (年 / 月 / 日,
  星期 / 週 / 曜日, 第二季度, 上半年), plus numeric dates (ISO, d/m/y, d.m.y, and m/d when the day is 13+ or a
  preposition precedes: "4/15", "on 7/8"; "3/4 of revenue" stays); years (1900–2099, also glued to letters or CJK:
  "EHA2025", "2025年"; not after a currency code: "USD2025") → "the year"; fiscal years → "the fiscal year";
  quarter labels in any case and with a year in any order ("Q2-25", "2025Q2", "FY2025Q2", "Q1 CY2025") → "the
  quarter" / "quarterly" (also "3. Quartal", "T3 2025"); half-years → "the half-year"; leading datelines and ISINs
  are removed; remnants of a masked range ("recently-18") lose the number. Period labels use ASCII word
  boundaries: CJK characters are word characters, so "年Q2" has no `\b`.
- **Price talk and price levels.** After NA-02's scrub runs again, a sentence with a stock / share price move in
  percent is dropped unless a fundamental noun is its subject ("revenue rose 5%" stays), and so is a sentence with
  a share-price level ("boosts stock to $102.89"; not "per diluted share at $0.23" or a dividend). Quote lines go
  ("Price: 152.75, Change: -15.87, Percent Change: -9.4%", "|Price:49.32|Chg%:-0.60"). Price targets: "to $X from
  $Y" becomes "raised / lowered by about N%" (or "slightly"), a lone amount is dropped ("set a price target"; a
  footnote mark after the amount, "$49.00.1", is ignored); a per-share deal or offering price becomes "a per-share
  price"; EPS, dividends and other per-share fundamentals stay.
- **Scanner** (`states/scan.py`; it imports nothing from the masker and does not read the alias file, tested).
  References: ATOM names and their distinctive words, names harvested from the unmasked texts right before the
  company's own "(TICKER)" (≥ 3 times), CJK names by its own rule (every 2–6 character ending of the CJK word
  before the ticker seen ≥ 2 times), the ATOM / vendor tickers in every form and **every bare ticker in capitals
  in mixed-case text** (no English-word list; in a text mostly in capitals only a ticker that is not an ordinary
  word of the corpus; a clock time "4:30 PM" excepted), its own date patterns in eleven languages, option
  expiries, period labels in any case and glued to text, years also after letters or CJK; plus, for the REPORT,
  price-move sentences, quotes (incl. quote widgets), price levels (incl. "Price:49.32") and public figures (a
  sentinel list and Chinese transliterated names). It scans one headline or snippet at a time. The scanner is
  conservative where the masker keeps a word on purpose: "IT spending" in Gartner's texts counts as a ticker. A one-word name counts as written when it
  is the whole ATOM name ("Target"), and otherwise only if it is not an ordinary lower-case word of the corpus (its
  own vocabulary), so headline words that precede a ticker ("Earnings", "Research") do not count.

  - month-year and month-day-year dates;

- **Revision 5, price talk (operator decision 2026-10-26: every move and every index or commodity level).**
  The verification also found percent price moves in about 2.3% of val-A D0 states: about 1.8% index or futures
  wraps (Barchart's "The S&P 500 Index ($SPX) (SPY) today is up +0.20%"), 0.2% other companies, 0.3% the
  company's own past moves (Zacks' "Shares have added about 8.5% in that time frame"); no own pre-market move.
  The operator chose to remove all of them, and index and commodity levels too. A sentence goes
  (`Masker.has_market_move`, run before NER by `drop_market_moves`, which would otherwise read "Gold Surges" as
  a person, and again in the final pass of `mask_prices`) when it holds:
  - an index level ("Dow 46,414", "closed at 6552", "the 20,000 mark"), a commodity or crypto amount behind a
    level word or a move verb or before a level noun ("Gold Surges to $3,816", "Bitcoin Retakes $112K", "a
    $3,400 Threshold", "$63.56 a barrel", "$90/b"), an amount before a move noun ("Nvidia’s $185 Rally"), a quote
    ("UpsideBX-1.48%");
  - a percent or points number with a move word just before it, a sign, or a move named after it (up to three
    words on: "a 4.38% recent decline", "Mastercard's 13% 3-Month Rise", "a 0.68% closing decline", "its first
    2% down day"), when the sentence holds no fundamental and names a market subject, the company or a ticker
    anywhere; or it is signed with no fundamental before it or right after it; or the noun after it names the
    share price or a trading period; or a possessive name or an idiom precedes it ("extends gain to 7.7%"); or
    a market subject, the company or a ticker follows the last fundamental within 90 characters before it and
    stands before its move word ("Strong earnings sent shares up 7%", "Micron Technology (MU) has seen a modest
    rise of 0.93%"); or a capitalized name that is no fundamental stands right before the move verb. Points need
    no move word when an index or other market subject is their subject ("the index ahead on the week by 180
    points"); a percent below or above a high is a move ("26% below its all-time high", "2% shy of the
    record").

  Fundamentals stay: revenue, margins, EPS, guidance, deliveries, macro gauges ("the PMI index", "the Consumer
  Price Index", "Pending Home Sales Index (PHSI)"), stakes and short interest ("Short Interest in Bank of America
  Co. (NYSE:BAC) Decreases By 8.1%"), payments ("CMS announced a 5.06% Medicare Advantage payment increase";
  agency acronyms are no tickers), a product's "5% price increase", forecasts ("Potential 10% Gain", "Projects
  52.5% S&P Rally") and the masker's own price-target output ("raised by about 7%", also when restated: "a 16%
  increase"). The scanner has every class in its own wording (`scan.market_move_count`).

  Four builds: each one was read in full by the scanner's hits, the verification's own date and price scripts,
  and listings of every month or weekday token, every index or commodity level and every percent with a move
  word; what they found that no rule had seen went into the next pass. Precision on articles before the test
  period, hand-read samples: about 90% of the dropped sentences are price talk (the rest mostly macro data,
  forecasts and product prices). About 2.3% of company and 11% of market articles before 2026 lose a sentence.

## States (S5; `states/build.py`)

- **Shape.** Canonical JSON (sorted keys, compact separators, UTF-8, `ensure_ascii=False`). Top-level keys
  are `attention, company, market, news` for Call A, plus `reaction_so_far` for Call B; market states have
  `market, news`. News items are {headline, snippet, source_type, timing}, in event-list order.
- **Text.** NA-02's scrubbed title / snippet as capped in the NA-03 event list, masked. The summary variant
  is not built: vendor summaries are text of the §11.6 ablation only (CLAUDE.md A6).
- **Excluded articles (revision 3; PREREG §15 NA-04 row 6).** Two kinds of article are in no state, in any
  window or call. The rule is `configs/states.yaml` `news_exclusion` on NA-02's raw fields
  (`build.excluded_ids`, applied in `news_items` and `market_selection`):
  - **Move explainers:** a title "Why … stock/shares … today", or StockStory's snippet "What Happened? Shares of
    / A number of …". They explain the session's move by construction. Of 204 pre-open-stamped explainers about
    the company itself (2024-01 … 2025-09), 29 describe a move after their stamp (18 the same session, 11 the
    next). DGX's D0 state for 2025-07-21 read "jumped 6.4% in the morning session" about 07-22.
  - **TradingView reposts:** every tradingview.com domain. Their vendor timestamps are batch times: 128 of 511
    of their earnings summaries are stamped before the release they report (other outlets 0–7%, previews
    included). DGX's 07-22 results sat in its 07-21 pre-open state under the same stamp as the explainer.

  The NA-03 lists are not refilled and the attention counts are unchanged. A W0 explainer is pre-market talk at
  best (§6.4), and a W2 one reveals the realized move, so neither kind is kept in any window. An event whose
  every listed article is excluded gets no state: 596 D0, 503 D2 and 224 Call B events. In all, 8,537 D0 and
  11,889 D2 listed articles are left out (`build_states.json`). The operator chose the TradingView rule over
  keeping the source with a disclosed risk. The masker's price-move filter also catches verbs glued to a
  ticker (Masking, revision 3).
- **Non-English articles (revision 4; operator decision B; PREREG §15 NA-04 row 8).** These are in no state
  either, with the same scope and the same no-refill rule as row 6. The rule is `build.non_english` on NA-02's
  raw title + snippet, configured in `configs/states.yaml` `news_exclusion.non_english`. A text is
  non-English when one of these holds:
  - it has two characters of a non-Latin script;
  - its distinct function words of other languages are at least three and outnumber its distinct English
    ones (two are enough when there is no English one);
  - it has three distinct words with a non-English letter (ä, é, ł, ř, ş, …) and at most two English function
    words.

  Details of the rule:
  - Words count in lower case or capitalized, never in capitals, so Deere's ticker DE is not "de".
  - "Las Vegas" and "Los Angeles / Gatos / Alamos / Cabos" are removed first.
  - Words are counted once, so a repeated name ("DuPont de Nemours") cannot add up.

  Why not reuse NA-02's flag: `non_english_hint` needs two words of a short list. It flagged 5.0% of event
  articles and missed 36% of the verification's non-English price levels and 47% of its Nordic and Dutch dates.

  Measured on the 299,138 masked articles:
  - the rule flags 7.7% of them;
  - it finds 98–100% of each residue class;
  - 205 sampled flags were all non-English: each development step's additions, and 40 flags on
    English-language domains.

  What it misses are short texts with few function words, such as a one-line Dutch headline in the val-A D0
  residue. The decision is applied here and not at NA-02's `keep`, because `keep` feeds the frozen F5 filter
  (NA-03).

  Effect: 22,960 of 296,082 event articles (7.75%) are non-English. 871 D0 events (1.81%) now have every listed
  article excluded, 254 of them only by this rule (596 before revision 4), and 814 D2 and 480 Call B events
  likewise.
- **Explainer titles (revision 4).** Row 6's title rule needed the singular "stock" or "shares", so "Why Micron
  Technology Rallied Today", "Why Airline Stocks Are Flying High Today" and "Why CoreWeave Rocketed as High as 26%
  Today" (188 event articles) had escaped. It now takes any move word or a percent between "why" and "today", but
  not StockStory's "Here's Why We Think F5 … Might Deserve Your Attention Today". Corpus explainers: 8,760 (7,535
  before).
- **Running market stories (revision 6; PREREG §15 NA-04 row 10).** Live blogs and running market reports are in no
  W0 state: Call A on D0, the NA-07 samples, the unmasked twin. The rule is `build.running_story_ids`
  (`configs/states.yaml` `news_exclusion.running_story`) on NA-02's raw title and snippet and `t_avail`.
  - **Why.** These pages are updated in place and keep their first vendor stamp, so their text reports the
    decision session's own cash market: AP's "U.S. stocks are drifting …" stamped 04:41 ET, Reuters' "The S&P 500
    ended Tuesday's choppy session" at 05:57 that Tuesday, Investing.com's "Stock market today: S&P 500 ends higher"
    stamped the evening before. Morning-stamped market headlines agreed with SPY's open-to-close direction of the
    session 57.1% of the time, evening-stamped ones 48.5% (train + val-A); after the rule, 50.8%.
  - **Five rules,** any one enough, s being the article's W0 session:
    1. a live format, whatever the stamp;
    2. a live entry or an update at or after the open of s, for an article stamped on the date of s (London time
       for a European market);
    3. a market close whose nearest weekday is that of s;
    4. running intraday wording about the market (a market subject, or a Reuters text), stamped that day or naming
       the weekday;
    5. a US-market wrap title stamped that day.

    Vetoes keep pre-open pieces, schedules, Asian closes, European mornings, forecasts and companies named like an
    index (Dow Inc., Nasdaq Inc., S&P Global, Live Nation).
  - **W0 only.** W2 ends at the close, so there the same text is inside the window: Call A on D2, Call B and the A3.5
    market states keep these articles and are byte-identical to revision 5. Each article has exactly one W0 session
    (`calendar.session_of_w0`), so the rule is an article-id set (`Inputs.excluded_w0`) applied by `news_items`
    for W0 specs only (`build.skipped`). The mask stage is unchanged.
  - **Company items stay.** A company's "shares rose 3.7% in early trading" stamped before the open is mostly
    pre-market (Coca-Cola, 08:11, after its 06:55 release), so rule 4 needs a market subject. A Reuters text is the
    exception: its style keeps "premarket" for before the open, so its "early trading" means after it.
  - **Effect.** 3,629 of 187,459 D0-listed articles; 3,447 article slots leave the D0 states; 190 more D0 events have
    no article left (1,061 in all). Many were vendor tags whose only article was a market page. The NA-03 lists are
    not refilled and the attention counts are unchanged, as in rows 6 and 8.
  - **Checked** by reading samples of every rule's hits, the full listing of morning-stamped market wraps left in,
    and the sixth verification's nets on the rebuilt states (val-A D0: its signatures 2.00% → 0.18%, every remaining
    hit a correct keep). An independent scanner count (`scan.post_open_count`) reads 4.69% → 0.01% of val-A D0
    states (one false positive).
- **Call A on D2 (A3.1)** uses the W2 event list and the W2 attention counts. It shares a hash with Call A on

- **Call B reaction (D2)** comes from `panel.reactions.build_reactions(period="public")`: G = z_cc,t, the
