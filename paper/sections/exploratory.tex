\section{Exploratory analyses (post hoc): where the signal lives}\label{sec:exploratory}

Everything in this section is exploratory. The analyses were requested after unblinding, they are outside every
pre-registered family and they support no claim; they suggest where to look, and the forward test of
Section~\ref{sec:limits} re-tests two of them as pre-registered hypotheses. Before this study was designed, the
authors' AI research assistant had looked at two of the statistics below on the test period (hit rates and mean
moves by conviction band, and the statistics of the 10:00 reading), and those looks shaped it. Every exploratory
variant is logged as a trial: the ledger grew from \res{ntrials-strat} to \res{ntrials-total} trials.

\paragraph{Earnings days carry the signal.} In a post hoc split, every signal's pre-open IC is concentrated on
the \res{earn-n-test} test events with a scheduled earnings report, and there it held in all three periods.
Laya-Fin's IC with $z_{cc}$ on earnings days was \res{lf-earn-vala}, \res{lf-earn-valb} and \res{lf-earn-test} on
val-A, val-B and the test, against \res{lf-rest-test} on the other test events; d1-ultra's was
\res{d1-earn-test} on the test ($t = \res{d1-earn-test-t}$). The fall from validation to test of
Section~\ref{sec:validation} is therefore a fall on the other events. Even on earnings days the signal is priced
at the open: d1-ultra's earnings-day IC on the test is \res{d1-earn-on-test} with $z_{on}$ and
\res{d1-earn-id-test} with $z_{id}$.

\paragraph{Selective prediction: the most confident calls carry most of the overnight signal.} We ranked events
post hoc by conviction, the size of $|\Nhat|$ against its val-A distribution, and kept only the top share of them,
which traces a coverage curve \citep{geifman2017} (Figure~\ref{fig:selective}). d1-ultra's mean overnight move in
the direction of its reading, $\mathrm{sign}(\Nhat)\cdot z_{on}$, is \res{sel-d1-on-10-test} typical moves in its
top 10\% on the test, against \res{sel-d1-on-all-test} for all events, and the pattern held in both validation
quarters. After the open, selectivity gains little: no test interval of the top share's mean move after the open
excludes zero at any coverage.

\paragraph{Watching the open adds nothing.} In a post hoc comparison, the reading at 10:00, after the first
half-hour bar, never predicts the rest of the day better than the pre-open reading on val-B or the test, and the
revision from the pre-open to the 10:00 reading has a zero or negative IC with the move from 10:00 to the close
(Laya-Fin \res{watch-lf-rev-test} on the test, $t = \res{watch-lf-rev-test-t}$). The models seem to over-update
toward the first bar, but the comparison involves many statistics, so this exploratory pattern is weak evidence.

\paragraph{Validation-to-test decay, the strongest instance.} A post hoc conditioning study asked whether the
after-the-open signal $\mathrm{sign}(\Nhat)\cdot z_{id}$ is stronger under some condition (the type and timing of
the news, the day, the stock, the models' internals, agreement between models and a cost hurdle) for
\res{cond-pairs} (model, condition) pairs. A strict rule, fixed before its test run, selected the pairs positive
with $t \ge 1$ in both validation quarters and a pooled $t \ge 2$, and \res{cond-selected} passed. On the test,
\res{cond-reversed} of them changed sign and the other \res{cond-positive} are small (Appendix
Figure~\ref{fig:a-conditioning}). Their tradable versions had in-sample gross Sharpe ratios up to
\res{cb-valb-gross-max} on val-B; on the test, none reaches the 90th percentile of its placebo, and every one loses
net of 10 basis points.
