\section{Clean validation did not survive the blinded test}\label{sec:validation}

Figure~\ref{fig:validation-test} compares each model's daily IC with the close-to-close move on val-B, the
quarter used for the final selection, with its IC on the test period. Laya-Fin was selected as the primary
instrument because its val-B IC, \res{lf-ic-valb} ($t = \res{lf-ic-valb-t}$), was the highest among the
candidates eligible for that role; on val-A, which it was not trained on, its IC had been \res{lf-ic-vala}. On the
test it fell to \res{lf-ic-test} ($t = \res{lf-ic-test-t}$), below the lower end of its val-B 95\% interval
(\res{lf-ic-valb-lo}). The fall is not specific to the fine-tuned model. Every model whose val-B IC was positive
fell: the commercial models' ICs roughly halved (d1-ultra's from \res{d1-ic-valb} to \res{d1-ic-test}), and the
generative LLM's fell from \res{llm-ic-valb} to \res{llm-ic-test} on its subsample. The clean price-only and
embedding benchmarks are near zero in both periods. The highest test ICs belong to the two baselines built on
vendor labels (\res{tags-ic-test} for the tag model); because the vendor produced those labels after the fact, we
read them as an upper bound that may contain look-ahead, not as a benchmark. Appendix
Table~\ref{tab:a-model-comparison} lists every model. A validation that looked clean on two quarters was weak
evidence of what the model would do next; an exploratory split (Section~\ref{sec:exploratory}) locates the decline
away from earnings days.

Table~\ref{tab:primary} reports the five primary hypotheses. None is supported for Laya-Fin (Holm-adjusted $p$
from \res{lf-h3-holm} to \res{lf-h1-holm}). H1 and H1b, whether the news is priced at the open, are the subject of
Section~\ref{sec:priced} and Table~\ref{tab:ic-target}, and H2, the over/under regression, of
Section~\ref{sec:overunder} and Table~\ref{tab:over-under}. H3 asks whether the
post-reaction judgment $J$ adds to price-only and news-only features in predicting $y_2$, the move from the next
open to the close of $t+5$; the gain in IC is \res{lf-h3} ($p = \res{lf-h3-p}$). H4 asks whether the readings of
materiality, surprise and expected magnitude predict the size of the day's move, $|z_{cc}|$, beyond a
heterogeneous autoregressive model of realized volatility \citep{corsi2009}, an earnings-day flag and the size
tier; they add \res{lf-h4} to the out-of-sample $R^2$ ($p = \res{lf-h4-p}$). Laya-Fin's magnitude head ranks the
size of the move (an AUC of \res{lf-auc} for moves of at least one typical day), so the models know which stocks
will move a lot, but no better than the stocks' own recent volatility.
