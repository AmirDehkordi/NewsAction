\section{The models cannot tell over- from under-reaction}\label{sec:overunder}

\subsection{The over/under regression}\label{sec:h2}

The central pre-registered hypothesis, H2, asks whether a model's reading of the news separates the part of a
day's move that will persist from the part that will reverse. After the close (D2) we regress the next week's
move on the news-implied move and on the day's move:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:h2}
y_{i,t} = a + b\,\Nhat_{i,t} + c\,G_{i,t} + \alpha_{d(t)\times\tau(i)} + \varepsilon_{i,t},
\end{equation}
where $y_{i,t} = y_2$ is the abnormal move from the next open to the close of $t+5$ in typical-move units,
$\Nhat_{i,t}$ is the model's reading of the day's news (all of it, up to the close, with price language removed),
$G_{i,t} = z^{cc}_{i,t}$ is the day's move and $\alpha_{d(t)\times\tau(i)}$ is a day-by-tier fixed effect; there is
no conditioning on the size of $G$. H2 predicts $b > 0$ (moves the news explains persist: underreaction to news)
and $c < 0$ (moves the news does not explain reverse: overreaction to price); both must hold, and each is tested
one-sided with standard errors clustered by date and firm.

Table~\ref{tab:over-under} reports the estimates. For Laya-Fin, $b = \res{lf-h2-b-d2}$ ($p = \res{lf-h2-bp-d2}$)
and $c = \res{lf-h2-c-d2}$ ($p = \res{lf-h2-cp-d2}$) on \res{lf-h2-n-d2} events. The reversal coefficient has the
predicted sign for every model but is never significant, and the news coefficient is not positive: d1-ultra's is
\res{d1-h2-b-d2} ($p = \res{d1-h2-bp-d2}$ for a positive effect). The two earlier decision points, reported
outside the family, do not change the picture. None of the eight pre-registered heterogeneity cuts (the three size
tiers, quantified against story news, small moves, press wires, low usual coverage and busy days) changes
Laya-Fin's coefficients (Benjamini--Hochberg $q \geq \res{het-q-min}$; Appendix Table~\ref{tab:a-heterogeneity}),
and neither does the style of the coverage: a more promotional or sensational tone, a dimension of text separate
from its content \citep{wegmann2026}, does not predict reversal (H6; Appendix Table~\ref{tab:a-secondary}).

\subsection{Convergence to the news-implied value}\label{sec:convergence}

The convergence analysis asks the same question along the whole path from the open to $t+20$. For each rung
$h$ of a ladder from the open to the close of $t+20$, we regress the level move from the previous close to $h$
on the news-implied value:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ladder}
\CARlvl_{i,h} = \kappa_h V_i + \alpha_{d(t)\times\tau(i)} + \varepsilon_{i,h},
\qquad h \in \{O_t,\ \text{10:00},\ C_t,\ C_{t+1},\ C_{t+2},\ C_{t+5},\ C_{t+10},\ C_{t+20}\},
\end{equation}
where $\CARlvl_{i,h}$ is the cumulative abnormal return in typical daily moves, without square-root-of-time
scaling, so that it is comparable with $V$. $\kappa_h = 1$ means that by rung $h$ the price has moved all the way
to the model's value; a rising $\kappa_h$ means drift toward it and a falling one means reversal.

Prices move part of the way to the model's value on day $t$ and no further (Figure~\ref{fig:convergence};
Appendix Table~\ref{tab:convergence}). For Laya-Fin, $\kappa_h$ is \res{lf-kappa-ct} at the close and
\res{lf-kappa-t20} at $t+20$; for d1-ultra the path is higher and has the same shape, from \res{d1-kappa-ct} at
the close to \res{d1-kappa-t20} at $t+20$. Neither pre-registered increase from the open to $t+20$ is significant
($p = \res{lf-kappa-incr-p}$ for Laya-Fin and \res{d1-kappa-incr-p} for d1-ultra). Prices also drift in $V$'s
direction before the event: five sessions earlier, d1-ultra's top decile stood \res{d1-dec10-pre5} and its bottom
decile \res{d1-dec1-pre5} relative to the previous close. The states describe the company's recent abnormal
performance in words, so part of $V$ reflects momentum.

Because $V$ is a model's estimate, a slope below one does not by itself mean that the market underreacts. Under
classical measurement error, the level of $\kappa_h$ mixes market efficiency with model noise, while the shape of
the ladder (a rise or a fall after the decision point) is identified up to a positive factor
(Appendix~\ref{app:convergence}). The informative fact is therefore the fade after the close: the part of the
day's move that lines up with the news does not continue, and partly gives back, although the pre-registered
increase is not significant, so the fade is descriptive. The gap between the model's value and the price does not
close either: its half-life is not reached within 20 trading days for any model, and because $V$ is small next to
the day's move, the pre-registered gap-closure test measures how much of the day's move reverses, not convergence
to $V$ (Appendix~\ref{app:convergence}).

\subsection{Under, over and the size of the move}\label{sec:rg}

The post-reaction reading classifies each event as an underreaction, a fair reaction or an overreaction, by how
far the day's move fell short of or exceeded the model's value. Figure~\ref{fig:over-under}~(a) follows
d1-ultra's classes after the close, signed in the direction of the news. The \res{rg-under-n} ``Under'' events
moved against the news on day $t$ and never catch up (\res{rg-under-t20} typical moves at $t+20$, against a mean
value of \res{rg-under-v}); the ``Over'' events give back part of their move, from \res{rg-over-t0} at the close
to \res{rg-over-t20}. Laya-Fin calls too few events either way to draw. Panel (b) asks the question by the size of
the move instead: moves of at least three typical days reverse slightly in every tercile of news strength (two of
the three intervals include zero), and smaller moves show small means of both signs. There is no sign that
important news with a small move is
underpriced.

Two descriptive cuts, pre-registered for reporting but not as tests, show where convergence is strongest.
Laya-Fin's slope at the close is \res{lf-kappa-ct-top100} for the 100 largest companies against
\res{lf-kappa-ct-mid} and \res{lf-kappa-ct-rest} for the other tiers. And by d1-ultra's own label of the news,
prices at $t+20$ reflect the value of numerical news ($\kappa_{t+20} = \res{d1-kappa-t20-numbers}$,
$p = \res{d1-kappa-t20-numbers-p}$) but not that of narrative news (\res{d1-kappa-t20-narrative}), a pattern that
echoes the earlier finding that markets treat numbers and stories differently \citep{kargarzadeh2026}.
