\section{Pre-open news is priced at the open}\label{sec:priced}

Equation~\eqref{eq:split} splits each day's move at the open. Figure~\ref{fig:priced-open} and
Table~\ref{tab:ic-target} show on which side of the open the models' readings predict it. For the three
commercial models the answer is clear. Their daily IC with the overnight move $z_{on}$ is \res{d1-ic-on-test}
(d1-ultra), \res{span-ic-on-test} (span-02) and \res{drex-ic-on-test} (drex-v2.0), with $t$-statistics of
\res{d1-ic-on-test-t}, \res{span-ic-on-test-t} and \res{drex-ic-on-test-t}; their IC with the move after the
open, $z_{id}$, is \res{d1-ic-id-test}, \res{span-ic-id-test} and \res{drex-ic-id-test}. H1, the difference, is
\res{d1-h1} for d1-ultra (95\% interval \res{d1-h1-lo} to \res{d1-h1-hi}, $p = \res{d1-h1-p}$), and the same to
three decimals for the other two. Sorted by d1-ultra's reading, the top quintile of a day's events moves
\res{d1-qspread-on} typical daily moves more overnight than the bottom quintile ($t = \res{d1-qspread-on-t}$).
The untuned Laya
model shows the same pattern at a smaller scale (H1 $= \res{lz-h1}$, $p = \res{lz-h1-p}$). Because the
commercial models failed the screens, these results are model-specific.

No model predicts the move after the open. H1b, the IC with $z_{id}$, is not significant for any model on the
test: \res{lf-ic-id-test} for Laya-Fin ($t = \res{lf-ic-id-test-t}$), and between \res{lz-ic-id-test} and
\res{drex-ic-id-test} for the others. This matters for Laya-Fin, whose validation signal seemed to reach past
the open: on val-B its IC with $z_{id}$ was \res{lf-ic-id-valb} ($t = \res{lf-ic-id-valb-t}$), as large as its
IC with $z_{on}$ (\res{lf-ic-on-valb}). That is why H1b was added to the pre-registration before the freeze. On
the test, both of Laya-Fin's ICs are small (\res{lf-ic-on-test} and \res{lf-ic-id-test}) and neither hypothesis
is supported.

\paragraph{Robustness.} No pre-registered robustness variant changes these conclusions
(Appendix Figure~\ref{fig:a-robustness}): dollar-volume tiers, dropping articles with date-only or
hour-rounded time stamps, a non-overlapping sample, placebo-adjusted targets, a 60-session volatility, dropping
delisting horizons and dropping text about price moves. Two variants speak to when the news arrives. For
d1-ultra, the overnight IC is largest when the latest article arrives between 08:00 and 09:15
(\res{d1-ic-on-0815}, $t = \res{d1-ic-on-0815-t}$), smaller for 04:00 to 08:00 (\res{d1-ic-on-0408}) and not
significant when the latest article precedes 04:00 (\res{d1-ic-on-pre04}, $t = \res{d1-ic-on-pre04-t}$). For
Laya-Fin, the IC with $z_{cc}$ is \res{lf-ic-cc-pre04} when the latest article precedes 04:00 and
\res{lf-ic-cc-0408} and \res{lf-ic-cc-0815} in the two later windows. Unmasking does not raise d1-ultra's IC: on
\res{unmask-n} test events its IC with $z_{cc}$ is \res{unmask-ic-masked} with masked states and
\res{unmask-ic-unmasked} with the same states unmasked. The market reads the news before the open, and for
d1-ultra the closer to the open the news arrives, the more of the overnight move its reading predicts.
