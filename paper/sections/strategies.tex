\section{Economic significance}\label{sec:strategies}

The pre-registered strategies turn each reading into a daily long-short book within size tiers, hedged against
the market with SPY. The \emph{convergence strategy} (S-CONV) buys the events whose price lies furthest below the
model's value (the top 20\% of the gap $U$ between the value and the move so far) and sells those furthest above
it, after the first half hour (D1) or after the close (D2). Three more families rank the pre-open reading,
entering at the open (S-D0), and the post-reaction judgment $J$, signed by the move, at 10:00 (S-D1) and after the
close (S-D2). An RG book trades only the events the model calls an under- or over-reaction, and a gated book only
its most confident readings. Costs are charged at 5, 10 and 20 basis points per side on both legs and on the
hedge. Each book is compared with 100 books built on random signals over the same events, and its Sharpe ratio is
deflated \citep{bailey2014} for the \res{ntrials-strat} trials the ledger held when the strategies were evaluated:
every selection of filters, encodings, models and baselines, and every strategy variant up to then
(Appendix~\ref{app:dsr}).

No pre-registered strategy is worth trading after costs (Table~\ref{tab:strategies} and
Figure~\ref{fig:strategies}). Gross of costs, no book reaches the 95th percentile of its random-signal placebo,
and Laya-Fin's post-reaction book after the close falls below the 5th (percentile \res{sd2j-pct}), as expected
of judgments that carry no positive information about the next week. Net of 10 basis points every main book
loses except the tiny RG book, which holds one stock a side on a handful of days. The convergence strategy is, in
effect, a price-only gap fade: because the model's value is small next to the day's move, ranking by the gap buys
the day's losers and sells its winners. The two books share \res{sconv-shared} of their positions, and their
daily returns correlate \res{sconv-corr}. The highest gross Sharpe ratio, \res{sd0-gated-gross} for the pre-open
book restricted to Laya-Fin's most confident readings and held to the next close, breaks even at
\res{sd0-gated-be} basis points per side, and the one-day books turn over about \res{sd0-turnover} times their
capital a day, so costs decide their sign. The largest deflated-Sharpe probability of any test book is
\res{dsr-max}. Short-sale borrow costs, which would lower every net figure, are not modeled.
