\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

AI decision models read financial news well enough to line up with how a stock opens, and not well enough to
forecast what it does after that. A fine-tuned open-weights model that looked convincing on two validation
quarters did not survive a blinded test. The commercial models kept a signal, but only in the overnight gap,
where the market had already priced the news before anyone could trade on it at the open, as an efficient market
would \citep{fama1970}. The models could not tell an overreaction from an underreaction, their calls of
underreaction never caught up, and no pre-registered strategy survived costs and selection. Out of sample, news
explains a fraction of a percent of a stock's own daily variance, and the commercial models' stated
probabilities overstate their accuracy.

Three recommendations follow for anyone who backtests language-model readings of news. First, split the return at
the open: a pre-open signal can be traded only if it predicts the move after the open, and a close-to-close
backtest credits it with a move nobody could trade. Second, measure recognition, not just masking: report how
often the model names the masked company against a matched baseline, and where possible test on news published
after the model's release, as our pre-registered forward test will. Third, freeze and count: select on validation
data only, freeze the plan by hash, and log every trial and deflate for them; the strongest exploratory pattern we
found, with in-sample Sharpe ratios up to \res{cb-valb-gross-max}, reversed on the test. The market reads the news
first.
